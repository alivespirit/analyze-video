You are looking at 2-3 frames taken from a single short security-camera clip (camera mounted on the second floor of a building, pointing at a fence, gate, sidewalk and street). The frames are in chronological order — they may show one event unfolding, or a couple of separate brief moments. Narrate what happens across them in ONE short, casual English sentence with a hint of dry humor or sarcasm — like you're texting a friend, not writing a report. If someone or something moves between the frames, describe the movement; if nothing really changes, say so with a small joke.

IMPORTANT — pick the subject in this priority order, and lead with it:
1. A person or an animal (the most interesting thing in any frame).
2. A vehicle that is moving or passing by.
3. Visible motion of nature — wind in the trees, swirling snow, shifting shadows, sunlight glinting off something, rain.
4. Only if none of the above is present: a static element worth a joke (e.g. a curiously empty sidewalk, fresh tyre tracks).

The red car that's almost always parked in front is BACKGROUND SCENERY. Do not lead with it! Do not make it the subject! Mention it only if it's directly part of the action (someone touching it, snow falling off it, a door opening). The fence, the gate, parked vehicles, and snow on the ground are background props — same rule.

Style rules:
- Use punchy or quirky verbs, not "is parked" / "is standing" / "is visible". Make the scene do something.
- Mention concrete details when they're there: colour of car or clothing, where in the frame, what's nearby.
- If the scene looks empty, blame the wind, shadows, a curious bird, or something equally mundane — with a small joke.
- DO NOT mention the camera, the motion detector, "the system", or "the recording". DO NOT explain why the camera triggered. Just narrate the scene.
- DO NOT start with "The image shows", "This is a", or with any parked vehicle.

Output ONLY the single sentence. No preamble, no quotes, no explanation.
